## Step 2 writing-profile guidance

The writing profile asks visitors to describe:

- what they need to write;
- the reader and purpose;
- what currently feels difficult;
- whether the need is general writing or a named examination/syllabus route.

This content is a self-description aid. It is not a scored diagnostic, formal placement,
proficiency level, CEFR assessment or document review. It collects no writing sample and produces
no automated result.

## Step 2 development framework

The public framework groups possible priorities into:

1. purpose, reader and task;
2. sentence control;
3. paragraph focus and development;
4. overall organisation and cohesion;
5. vocabulary, tone and precision;
6. revision and self-review.

These areas are a learner-readable map of connected writing concerns. They are not evidence of a
fixed module order, lesson count, package inclusion, feedback method or equal coverage for every
learner. Public copy must continue to use `possible priorities`, `may` and context-led language
until an owner-approved programme scope is recorded in
`docs/english-writing-offer-verification.md`.

No CEFR level descriptors are copied or published. The page does not claim CEFR alignment or that
Aisha is a certified CEFR assessor.

## Context examples

Study-related, workplace and everyday writing examples are recognition cues. They do not confirm
that every document type is accepted or taught.

- Study guidance supports the learner's own writing development and never completion of assessed
  work.
- Workplace guidance requires the exact document type and confidentiality needs to be confirmed
  before any material is shared.
- Everyday-writing guidance remains broad because practical written communication takes many
  forms.

Dissertations, theses, journal papers, admissions materials, named citation styles and specialist
business-document services remain unverified and must not be inferred from these examples.

## Editing, feedback and authorship boundary

`Revision and self-review` describes a learner skill. It does not promise proofreading, line
editing, rewriting, unlimited corrections, detailed feedback or a turnaround time.

The public scope statement is limited to this principle: coaching helps learners develop and revise
their own writing; it does not complete assessed work, disguise plagiarism or help someone
misrepresent authorship.

Detailed academic-integrity, AI-use, document-storage, privacy, feedback and editing policies still
require owner confirmation before publication or feature development.
